# Peru local-business automation market: broad opportunity scan

**Research date:** 15 July 2026  
**Corrected decision:** Which expanding local-market sector in Peru has a costly recurring operational problem that can support a practical monthly automation service?  
**Important correction:** Booking is one possible workflow, not the assumed solution. The earlier booking-only report remains a separate, narrower study.

## Executive answer

The best first wedge is an **inquiry-to-estimate follow-up assistant for independent automotive workshops** in Lima. It should capture a vehicle/customer inquiry from WhatsApp, collect the minimum job details, create a structured estimate request, remind staff when a lead is unanswered, send an approved estimate, follow up, record acceptance/rejection, and request a deposit or appointment only after the estimate is accepted. It should not diagnose vehicles, price repairs autonomously, manage inventory, or replace the workshop's invoicing system.

This conclusion is not based on registrations. INEI reports that maintenance and repair of motor vehicles grew **11.7% year-on-year in Q4 2025**, after growth of **7.3% in Q1 2025**; that is real activity evidence, although it is not a workshop-only revenue series ([INEI, 2025 annual/Q4 GDP release](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/inei/noticias/1357291-producto-bruto-interno-aumento-3-2-en-el-cuarto-trimestre-de-2025-y-durante-el-ano-2025-acumulo-un-crecimiento-de-3-4), [INEI, Q1 2025](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/inei/noticias/1173385-pbi-crecio-3-9-en-el-primer-trimestre-de-2025)). Workshops also have a more monetizable failure than merely “administrative work”: a slow or forgotten estimate can lose a repair order worth hundreds or thousands of soles. Whether this leakage is common enough to support a subscription is still an assumption to validate from real lead logs.

The next two opportunities are:

1. **Small transport/logistics operators:** job-to-proof-of-delivery document capture and invoice-readiness.
2. **Construction subcontractors/specialty contractors:** inquiry-to-quote, site evidence and progress-certificate document control.

## Evidence discipline

- **Evidence** means an official Peruvian statistic, regulation/service page, or a first-party vendor page.
- **Inference** is a reasoned interpretation of evidence; it is not directly measured.
- **Assumption** is a commercial or workflow hypothesis that must be tested before building.

Official public data do **not** establish Peru-wide rates for missed WhatsApp leads, late estimates, no-shows, stockouts, late invoices, manual hours, software penetration, or willingness to pay in these micro-sectors. No recommendation below treats those unknowns as facts.

At year-end 2024 Peru had 2,346,592 formal firms; 99.1% were MYPE, and commerce plus services represented 85.8% of the MYPE base ([PRODUCE](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/produce/noticias/1168972-produce-peru-cerro-el-2024-con-2-34-millones-de-empresas-formales)). This establishes a large owner-operated market, not automatic SaaS readiness. Digital distribution is plausible: OSIPTEL reports 96% of households had fixed or mobile internet and 95.4% had a smartphone in 2025 ([OSIPTEL ERESTEL 2025](https://www.osiptel.gob.pe/portal-del-usuario/noticias/erestel-2025-aumenta-a-96-los-hogares-peruanos-que-tienen-acceso-a-internet-fijo-o-movil/)). Those are household indicators, not proof that a business uses WhatsApp formally or will grant API access.

## Sector analyses

### 1. Independent automotive workshops

1. **Market growth and demand — evidence:** the broader motor-vehicle sale, maintenance and repair category grew 6.95% over full-year 2025; maintenance/repair grew 11.7% year-on-year in Q4 and 7.3% in Q1 ([INEI full technical report, pp. 32–33](https://m.inei.gob.pe/media/MenuRecursivo/boletines/02-informe-tecnico-produccion-nacional-diciembre-2025.pdf), [INEI Q4](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/inei/noticias/1357291-producto-bruto-interno-aumento-3-2-en-el-cuarto-trimestre-de-2025-y-durante-el-ano-2025-acumulo-un-crecimiento-de-3-4), [INEI Q1](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/inei/noticias/1173385-pbi-crecio-3-9-en-el-primer-trimestre-de-2025)). This category includes vehicle sales and does not quantify independent workshops or prove a durable multi-year rate.
2. **Customer behavior/digital adoption — evidence/inference:** high household smartphone/connectivity supports message-led discovery and follow-up ([OSIPTEL](https://www.osiptel.gob.pe/portal-del-usuario/noticias/erestel-2025-aumenta-a-96-los-hogares-peruanos-que-tienen-acceso-a-internet-fijo-o-movil/)); the actual share of inquiries by WhatsApp, Facebook, phone and walk-in must be measured.
3. **Main operational pain — assumption:** inquiries arrive without plate/model/symptoms; staff interrupt technicians to price; estimates are assembled slowly; follow-ups are forgotten; customers repeatedly ask for status.
4. **Current handling — assumption:** WhatsApp chats, calls, paper job cards and spreadsheets. Verify in 15 workshops, not from anecdotes.
5. **Competitors/substitutes — vendor evidence:** generic CRMs such as [Kommo](https://www.kommo.com/) manage messaging pipelines; [Alegra Peru](https://www.alegra.com/peru/) and [Nubefact](https://www.nubefact.com/) address accounting/e-invoicing. These validate adjacent spend but do not prove workshop adoption. Paper, receptionist labor and WhatsApp Business are substitutes.
6. **Automation opportunities:** lead capture, missing-field prompts, staff SLA alerts, estimate templates, approval/follow-up, deposit link, status updates, maintenance reminders; later, parts-request and margin reporting.
7. **Willingness to pay — assumption:** medium-high for 4+ technicians or 80+ monthly inquiries if one recovered repair covers the fee. Test with payment, not stated interest.
8. **Sales difficulty — inference:** medium. Owner/managers are locally reachable; trust is easier if the product sits beside current WhatsApp rather than replacing it.
9. **MVP complexity:** medium-low if the workshop approves every price. High if it attempts diagnosis, parts catalogs, ERP, SUNAT invoicing or insurer claims.
10. **Best first use case:** prevent every qualified WhatsApp inquiry and estimate from going unanswered for more than a configurable interval.

### 2. Small freight, courier and last-mile operators

1. **Growth — evidence:** transport, storage, postal and courier activity grew 5.0% in 2025; Q1 2025 grew 7.4%, including 9.0% for storage/post/courier ([INEI annual](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/inei/noticias/1357291-producto-bruto-interno-aumento-3-2-en-el-cuarto-trimestre-de-2025-y-durante-el-ano-2025-acumulo-un-crecimiento-de-3-4), [INEI Q1](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/inei/noticias/1173385-pbi-crecio-3-9-en-el-primer-trimestre-de-2025)). This aggregate includes passenger and large-enterprise activity; it is not a last-mile SME revenue measure.
2. **Digital adoption:** national connectivity is strong; operational adoption among small fleets is unmeasured.
3. **Pain — assumption:** order details copied from chats, driver assignment, missing proof-of-delivery (POD), repeated ETA questions, and delayed invoicing because evidence is incomplete.
4. **Current handling — assumption:** WhatsApp groups, calls, photos and spreadsheets; validate by shadowing dispatchers.
5. **Competition/substitutes:** full transport-management systems, GPS/fleet platforms, WhatsApp groups and clerical staff. SUNAT already provides electronic invoices and electronic shipping documents, so a new service should orchestrate evidence rather than recreate tax infrastructure ([SUNAT SEE-SOL](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/sunat/pages/7332-sistema-de-emision-electronica-see-sol), [SUNAT electronic document types](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/sunat/pages/26395-tipos-de-comprobantes-electronicos)).
6. **Opportunities:** structured order intake, dispatch board, driver task messages, POD capture/validation, exception alerts, customer ETA messages and invoice-readiness packet.
7. **WTP — assumption:** high when billing is delayed or disputed, but small fleets may have thin margins.
8. **Sales difficulty:** medium-high due to operational risk, fragmented workflows and integration requests.
9. **MVP complexity:** medium. A single customer-specific POD workflow is feasible; route optimization and telematics are not.
10. **Best first use case:** collect the required POD photo/signature/reference and notify billing when a job is complete.

### 3. Construction specialty contractors

1. **Growth — evidence:** construction output grew 6.67% in 2025. INEI attributes activity to private residential/commercial/industrial works and infrastructure as well as public works; local-government public construction grew 19.56% ([INEI technical report, pp. 30–31](https://m.inei.gob.pe/media/MenuRecursivo/boletines/02-informe-tecnico-produccion-nacional-diciembre-2025.pdf)). Public procurement and large projects should not be mistaken for uniform growth among small contractors.
2. **Digital behavior — unknown:** smartphone access is high nationally, but tool adoption among electricians, HVAC, metalwork, fit-out and maintenance contractors must be measured.
3. **Pain — assumption:** site visits produce scattered photos/measurements; estimates are slow; scope changes lack an audit trail; progress evidence and payment certificates are assembled manually.
4. **Current handling — assumption:** phone/WhatsApp, Excel estimates, shared folders and paper sign-offs.
5. **Competition/substitutes:** project-management, field-service and accounting systems, plus administrative labor. Generic tools are plentiful; a niche template and easy evidence capture are the opening.
6. **Opportunities:** site-intake checklist, estimate document assembly, approval/follow-up, daily photo log, change-order record, progress evidence packet and collections reminders.
7. **WTP — assumption:** medium-high where contract value is meaningful and documentation gates payment.
8. **Sales difficulty:** medium-high; workflows vary by specialty and public-sector suppliers face tender/compliance complexity.
9. **MVP complexity:** medium for one specialty's quote/evidence packet; high for scheduling crews, costing, BIM or public procurement.
10. **Best first use case:** turn a structured site visit into a human-approved estimate, then preserve approved scope changes and payment evidence.

### 4. Private clinics and dental centers

1. **Demand — evidence:** RENIPRESS provides the official establishment registry; a 2022 MINSA release counted 14,669 private facilities among 24,158 registered IPRESS, but that older count must be refreshed directly from RENIPRESS before sizing ([SUSALUD RENIPRESS](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/susalud/campa%C3%B1as/95866-consulta-el-renipress), [MINSA 2022 count](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/minsa/noticias/582570-aplicativo-renipress-est-disponible-para-identificar-los-establecimientos-de-salud-formales)). This is market structure, not growth evidence.
2. **Digital behavior — inference:** consumers are connected, and health booking tools exist; clinic-specific channel use is unmeasured.
3. **Pain — assumption:** reception overload, incomplete pre-visit data, cancellations, manual confirmation, rejected/late payment and repeated post-visit instructions.
4. **Current handling — assumption:** phone/WhatsApp, receptionist, practice software and marketplaces. Verify by specialty and clinic size.
5. **Competition:** [Doctoralia Peru](https://www.doctoralia.pe/) and [AgendaPro Peru](https://agendapro.com/pe) offer scheduling/communication; an MVP needs a narrower measurable wedge, not another calendar.
6. **Opportunities:** confirmations, consented intake, wait-list refill, balance reminders, lab/result-ready notification and administrative FAQs. Avoid clinical advice.
7. **WTP — assumption:** high because clinician time is valuable, but procurement and compliance increase sales cost.
8. **Sales difficulty:** high: health data, trust, role permissions and existing systems.
9. **MVP complexity:** medium-high even without diagnosis. Store the minimum data and get legal review.
10. **Best first use case:** confirmation plus human-approved wait-list refill for one specialty, optionally paired with balance reminders.

### 5. Tour operators and independent hotels

1. **Growth — evidence:** Peru received 3.81 million international visitors in Jan–Nov 2025, up 4.5% year-on-year but still only 78.6% of the same 2019 period; this is recovery, not evidence of a new high ([MINCETUR](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/mincetur/noticias/1315781-mincetur-mas-de-3-8-millones-de-visitantes-internacionales-arribaron-al-peru-entre-enero-y-noviembre)). Accommodation/restaurants grew 2.3% in 2025 ([INEI](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/inei/noticias/1357291-producto-bruto-interno-aumento-3-2-en-el-cuarto-trimestre-de-2025-y-durante-el-ano-2025-acumulo-un-crecimiento-de-3-4)).
2. **Behavior:** international guests expect rapid digital responses across languages and time zones—an inference to validate by inquiry logs.
3. **Pain — assumption:** repeated itinerary questions, slow quote assembly, channel fragmentation, follow-up across time zones and payment verification.
4. **Current handling — assumption:** OTA inboxes, email, WhatsApp, spreadsheets and property/booking systems.
5. **Competition:** OTAs, property-management systems and general CRMs; the substitute is staff handling each inquiry.
6. **Opportunities:** multilingual qualification, approved quote assembly, itinerary document generation, payment reminder and pre-arrival information.
7. **WTP — assumption:** medium-high where each converted tour or room package has meaningful gross profit; seasonality matters.
8. **Sales difficulty:** medium; operators are reachable but integrations and fear of wrong promises are barriers.
9. **MVP complexity:** medium if knowledge/quotes are constrained; high if it synchronizes inventory across OTAs.
10. **Best first use case:** answer and qualify direct inquiries, then produce a staff-approved quote and follow-up sequence.

### 6. Catering/event food operators

1. **Growth — evidence:** catering grew 9.07% in 2025, materially faster than restaurants overall, driven by corporate/social-event contracts and customized requirements ([INEI technical report, pp. 36–37](https://m.inei.gob.pe/media/MenuRecursivo/boletines/02-informe-tecnico-produccion-nacional-diciembre-2025.pdf)). The official aggregate does not size independent caterers.
2. **Behavior — inference:** inquiries and menus are naturally document/message-heavy; confirm actual channel mix.
3. **Pain — assumption:** leads arrive with incomplete guest/date/menu/service data; staff repeatedly cost custom menus; quote versions and follow-up are inconsistent; deposits and supplier deadlines are missed.
4. **Current handling — assumption:** WhatsApp/email, spreadsheets, templates and calendars.
5. **Competition/substitutes:** restaurant/event software, generic CRM/quotation tools and coordinator labor.
6. **Opportunities:** guided event brief, approved package rules, costed quote draft, version control, follow-up, deposit reminders and event checklist.
7. **WTP — assumption:** medium where several events/month have high ticket value.
8. **Sales difficulty:** medium; founder-led businesses are accessible, but customization can overwhelm a horizontal product.
9. **MVP complexity:** medium if constrained to one caterer's packages; high if it tries real-time ingredient costing and procurement.
10. **Best first use case:** convert a complete event brief into a staff-approved quote and follow-up sequence.

### 7. Restaurants and multi-location food service

1. **Growth — evidence:** restaurants grew 2.3% in 2025, while accommodation/restaurants together grew 3.9% in Q4 ([INEI](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/inei/noticias/1357291-producto-bruto-interno-aumento-3-2-en-el-cuarto-trimestre-de-2025-y-durante-el-ano-2025-acumulo-un-crecimiento-de-3-4)). This is moderate expansion, not “fast-growing.”
2. **Behavior/digital:** delivery/POS/consumer messaging are visible, but official sector-level software penetration is unavailable.
3. **Pain — assumption:** purchasing from multiple vendors, invoice capture, price variance, stockouts, waste and uncertain recipe cost; booking matters only for reservation-led venues.
4. **Current handling — assumption:** POS plus spreadsheets, paper counts and WhatsApp supplier orders.
5. **Competition/substitutes:** POS/restaurant systems, accountants, spreadsheets and managers. SUNAT's electronic invoice infrastructure exists and is free at the tax-document layer ([SUNAT](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/sunat/pages/7309-emitir-factura-electronica-sistema-de-emision-electronica-sol)).
6. **Opportunities:** parse supplier invoices, normalize item prices, flag price/quantity anomalies, create approval lists and weekly food-cost report.
7. **WTP — assumption:** medium for multiple locations or meaningful purchasing volume; weak for a small single outlet.
8. **Sales difficulty:** medium-high because owners are busy and existing POS expectations are broad.
9. **MVP complexity:** medium-high due to messy units, recipes, substitutions and invoice formats.
10. **Best first use case:** supplier-invoice capture and weekly purchase-price variance alerts—not reservations.

### 8. B2B wholesalers and distributors

1. **Growth — evidence:** commerce grew 3.6% in 2025, and wholesale grew 4.56% year-on-year in December ([INEI commerce](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/inei/noticias/1356718-sector-comercio-se-incremento-4-89-en-diciembre-de-2025)). These are broad categories; identify a product vertical before building.
2. **Behavior:** electronic invoicing is an established tax workflow. SUNAT states invoices under applicable regimes are electronic and credit invoices record outstanding amount, due dates and installments ([SUNAT](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/sunat/pages/7309-emitir-factura-electronica-sistema-de-emision-electronica-sol)).
3. **Pain — assumption:** WhatsApp quote requests, repeated price/stock questions, manual order entry, lost follow-up and overdue invoices.
4. **Current handling — assumption:** sales reps, WhatsApp, Excel, accounting/ERP and telephone collections.
5. **Competition:** accounting/ERP/CRM products such as [Alegra Peru](https://www.alegra.com/peru/), [Nubefact](https://www.nubefact.com/) and [Kommo](https://www.kommo.com/). The opening is orchestration around an existing system, not recreating it.
6. **Opportunities:** structured RFQ intake, quote draft, approval, follow-up, order entry handoff, due-date reminders and dispute routing.
7. **WTP — assumption:** medium-high if the tool shortens quote time or days-sales-outstanding; integration may dominate cost.
8. **Sales difficulty:** high without a vertical and existing-system connector.
9. **MVP complexity:** medium for one catalog; high for inventory/pricing sync across arbitrary ERPs.
10. **Best first use case:** approved quote follow-up plus payment reminder for one distribution vertical.

### 9. Beauty salons, wellness centers and premium barbers

1. **Demand — evidence boundary:** the prior report cited increasing business registrations, but registrations are business formation—not revenue, productivity or durable demand. Current broad official activity releases do not isolate these categories well enough to call them fast-growing.
2. **Behavior:** national messaging access is strong; actual business channel mix remains unknown.
3. **Pain — assumption:** repetitive price/availability inquiries, slow lead response, incomplete follow-up, no-shows, schedule changes and reactivation of dormant clients.
4. **Current handling — assumption/vendor framing:** WhatsApp Business, paper/calendar, receptionist and salon platforms.
5. **Competition:** [AgendaPro Peru](https://agendapro.com/pe) and [Fresha](https://www.fresha.com/es/pricing) already cover booking and communication, making a generic booking calendar crowded.
6. **Opportunities:** recover unanswered inquiries, qualify desired service, route to a person, then book/remind; later reactivation.
7. **WTP — assumption:** low-medium except multi-staff, higher-ticket establishments.
8. **Sales difficulty:** low-medium; local owners are reachable, but churn and price sensitivity may be high.
9. **MVP complexity:** low for lead recovery, medium for reliable multi-staff scheduling.
10. **Best first use case:** unanswered WhatsApp inquiry recovery—not “AI receptionist” as a broad promise.

## Top three opportunities

### 1. Workshop inquiry-to-estimate control

The value event is an accepted repair estimate, not a chatbot conversation. The MVP is sellable if it proves: shorter first-response time, fewer orphaned estimates and more accepted work orders.

### 2. Logistics POD-to-invoice workflow

The value event is a completed evidence packet that lets billing issue an invoice without chasing a driver. This has strong operational ROI but needs deeper per-client setup.

### 3. Contractor quote and site-evidence workflow

The value event is an approved quote or payment-ready progress packet. Construction has the strongest headline output growth in this scan, but specialty variation and document complexity make it a worse first build than the workshop wedge.

## Recommended MVP

### Problem statement

Independent workshops receive repair inquiries through chats and calls, but the information needed to estimate is incomplete and follow-up depends on a busy owner or service adviser remembering each conversation. A qualified lead can therefore wait too long, receive no estimate, or disappear after an estimate, with no reliable visibility into lost repair revenue. This is a **hypothesis**, not yet a measured Peru-wide fact.

### Solution statement

A WhatsApp-adjacent workflow assistant captures required vehicle/job details, creates an estimate task for a human, alerts staff when it is overdue, sends the human-approved estimate, follows up on a fixed schedule, records the outcome, and hands accepted work to the existing appointment/job-card process. It automates coordination and persistence, not technical diagnosis or commercial judgment.

### Ideal first customer profile

- Independent general-mechanics, tires/suspension, bodywork or air-conditioning workshop in Lima.
- Owner plus one service adviser and 4–12 technicians.
- At least roughly 80 inbound inquiries/month and average repair ticket above roughly S/250—**screening assumptions to test**, not market facts.
- Uses WhatsApp as a primary inquiry channel and currently lacks disciplined CRM follow-up.
- Can export or manually provide 30 days of anonymized lead outcomes.
- Owner can approve a four-week pilot and name one staff champion.

Avoid dealerships, insurer-controlled body shops, one-person mechanics and workshops demanding ERP/inventory replacement in v1.

### Core MVP features

1. Shared lead inbox or forwarding/import method that does not force a number migration during the pilot.
2. Configurable intake checklist: vehicle plate, make/model/year, symptom/service, location preference and photos.
3. Lead stages: new, awaiting customer data, awaiting estimate, estimate sent, accepted, lost/no response.
4. Timer and staff alert when a qualified lead or estimate is overdue.
5. Human-edited estimate message/template; no autonomous price creation.
6. Two configurable follow-ups with opt-out and immediate human takeover.
7. Acceptance/deposit/appointment handoff link or simple manual action.
8. Weekly report: response time, qualified leads, estimates sent, accepted/lost and attributable recovered value.
9. Audit log, role access and minimum-data retention controls.

### Features to avoid in v1

- Voice agent or automated outbound calling.
- Vehicle diagnosis, repair recommendations or automatic labor/parts pricing.
- Inventory, procurement, accounting, SUNAT invoicing or full workshop ERP.
- Parts-catalog and insurance integrations.
- Predictive maintenance models.
- Multi-branch/multi-tenant enterprise administration.
- Open-ended generative replies without approved templates and human fallback.
- “AI” positioning as the product; sell recovered estimates and response discipline.

## Pricing hypothesis for Peru

Pricing is a test design, not evidence of willingness to pay:

- **Paid validation pilot:** S/149 for 30 days, one number/inbox, setup included, message-provider charges capped or passed through transparently.
- **Starter after proof:** S/249/month for one location, up to three staff users and a defined monthly conversation allowance.
- **Growth hypothesis:** S/399/month for higher volume, more users, deposit/payment-link handoff and custom reporting.
- **Optional setup after pilot:** S/250–500 only when workflow/configuration is non-trivial.

Anchor the sale against the gross profit from one recovered repair, but ask for actual average ticket and gross-margin range. Do not claim a return on investment until accepted jobs can be attributed conservatively. The price must also be tested against the owner doing nothing, WhatsApp Business, a receptionist and generic CRM—not just against workshop software.

## Plan to win the first five paying customers

1. **Choose one micro-vertical and two Lima clusters.** Start with air-conditioning or tires/suspension workshops, where service requests are relatively structured. Build a list from Google Maps/field walks only after confirming the vertical has 20+ reachable prospects.
2. **Run 15 workflow interviews before coding.** Ask the owner/service adviser to replay yesterday's inquiries and estimates on screen. Count messages, response time, missing fields, unsent estimates, follow-ups and outcomes. Do not ask whether they “like AI.”
3. **Audit real logs with consent.** For five willing shops, anonymize a 14–30 day cohort. Establish a baseline: qualified inquiries, estimate latency, orphaned leads, acceptance and reasons lost.
4. **Sell a manual concierge pilot.** Use a lightweight shared board and approved message templates behind the scenes. Charge S/149 up front. The first acceptance gate is **three paid pilots from no more than 20 qualified pitches**.
5. **Offer a precise guarantee, not free software.** Example: “Every qualified inquiry will have an owner and next action within 15 minutes during your configured hours; every approved estimate will receive the agreed follow-up.” Do not guarantee sales.
6. **Acquire customers 1–2 directly.** Founder visits, screen-level workflow audit, one-week baseline, paid pilot.
7. **Acquire customers 3–4 by referral.** Give an existing customer one free month only after the referred workshop pays; avoid permanent discounts.
8. **Acquire customer 5 through one local supplier/association.** Partner with a parts distributor, training center or workshop network only after two case studies show measurable results.
9. **Convert only on evidence.** At day 30 show before/after response times, estimates sent, accepted jobs and conservative attributable value. Ask for S/249/month.

| Customers choose mostly on price/location | Tag loss reason on every estimate                    | Follow-up rarely changes decisions                                |

## Data that must be verified before building production software

1. Sector-specific counts and geographic density of formal independent workshops; do not substitute a broad vehicle-maintenance activity index or registrations for market size.
2. Multi-year durability of workshop activity growth, not only two 2025 quarters.
3. Inquiry channel mix, volume, response-time distribution and estimate turnaround by workshop type.
4. Percentage of inquiries that are qualified, priced, followed up, accepted and lost—and reasons for loss.
5. Average ticket, gross-profit range and value of one incremental accepted repair.
6. Existing use and satisfaction with WhatsApp Business, workshop software, CRM and invoicing tools.
7. Whether the production WhatsApp/API setup can coexist with the owner's current number and habits; provider policy and fees current at pilot date.
8. Owners' actual paid conversion and three-month retention at S/249, not survey intent.
9. Staff adoption, daily active usage and error/handoff rate.
10. Legal basis, consent language, retention and processor obligations under current Peruvian personal-data rules; obtain local counsel before production.
11. For logistics/health alternatives: real POD-to-invoice delay and privacy/system-integration requirements, respectively.

## Final recommendation

Do not build a broad “AI automation platform,” and do not choose a sector merely because its registrations increased. Run a paid, manual workshop pilot around a measurable revenue leak first. If real logs do not show frequent orphaned inquiries/estimates or owners will not pay, move to the logistics POD-to-invoice test rather than adding features.

Build this first because… workshop inquiry-to-estimate follow-up targets a recurring, high-value commercial handoff in a sector with current activity growth, can prove value with accepted repair orders, and can be piloted without autonomous diagnosis, voice agents, inventory replacement or a large integration project.
